# How old is this tree? — treatment

Style: Ukiyo-e (Lemo-Opuscar `ukiyoe`). Vertical 1080×1920, 24 fps, 10.0 s, **silent** (same format as the vertical tsunami film). Facts arrive as printed caption cartouches (burned in and exported as `.srt`). Sources: `FACTS.md`.

## Facts (only these)

1. A tree in a temperate climate adds one growth ring each year: pale wood in spring, a thin dark band in late summer.
2. To age it, count the dark rings from the centre (the pith) out to the bark.
3. The number of rings is the tree's age in years.

On screen, shortened to read in under 3 s each: **"One ring grows each year"** · **"Count dark rings, centre out"** · **"12 rings = 12 years old"**.

## Three candidate structures

- **A. The life of one tree.** A sapling grows for 12 years, then is cut. Warm, but it spends most of the 10 s before the method appears.
- **B. Alternate impressions.** One cross-section reprinted with more rings each time. Clear, but the "how" (counting) would be implied, not shown.
- **C. The log, its making, the count.** Open on a finished print of a felled cedar log, end on (the thumbnail and the question). Cut to the rings being *printed* one per year, block by block, while an inset shows the tree growing through the seasons. Slide along the scroll to a close-up of the cut face, where a pointer counts the dark rings from the pith out, a numeral stamped at each. Cut back to the log, now marked "12", and pull back.

**Chosen: C.** The medium is the subject: a woodblock print is carved wood, and printing colour block by block is the same gesture as a tree laying down one ring at a time (the native move "printing, block by block"). The counting beat is the method itself, step by step.

## Logline and arc

A felled log keeps its whole life on its cut face; count it and it tells you its age.
Setup: how old is this tree? Turn: every year leaves one pale and one dark band. Ending: counted out from the centre, twelve rings, twelve years.

## Benchmark

- Hiroshige's *Hundred Famous Views*: bold foreground cropping (the log end filling the lower sheet), small landscape behind. **Learn** cropping and scale. **Don't take** compositions.
- Koma-e (a small framed print set inside a print), used in many 19th-century series: our seasons inset. **Learn** the device only.
