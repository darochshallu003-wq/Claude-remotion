# Facts shown in the film, with sources

1. **One growth ring per year (temperate trees), pale earlywood + dark latewood.**
   Encyclopaedia Britannica, "tree ring" / "dendrochronology": in temperate regions a tree adds one growth ring a year; the light, wide part grows in spring and early summer (earlywood), the dark, dense part later in the season (latewood). https://www.britannica.com/science/dendrochronology
2. **Count the rings from the centre (pith) out to the bark to age the tree.**
   US Forest Service / NOAA educational material on tree rings: count the dark latewood bands from the pith to the bark; each ring is one year. https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/paleoclimatology/tree-ring
3. **Number of rings = age in years.**
   Same sources. Caveat (not shown, to keep to the three facts): rings counted on a stump give the age at that height, so the true age is slightly higher; some trees occasionally skip or double a ring, and tropical trees often lack clear annual rings.

The tree in the film is drawn with 12 rings (an illustration, not a real specimen).
