# The Goodbye Money — script (draft v1)

Vertical cartoon short, ~60 s, English, one steady narrator voice. No captions or labels on screen.

## Facts (each joke rests on one)
1. **Most grandparents give grandkids cash.** In a MetLife survey of US grandparents (2012), 82% said they give
   cash. Earlier MetLife survey (2009): 83%. UK, Saga (2019): about half of grandparents planned to give cash at
   Christmas, rising to 65% among those aged 80-89.
   - https://www.thinkadvisor.com/2012/09/11/grandparents-give-more-than-1600-a-year-to-support-grandkids-metlife/
   - https://www.metlife.com/about-us/newsroom/2009/july/grandparents-are-providing--370-7-billion-in-financial-support-t/
   - https://www.blackpoolgazette.co.uk/lifestyle/half-grandparents-will-give-grandchildren-cash-christmas-1347603
2. **Shagun amounts end in one (101, 501, 1001).** Folk explanations, no single official origin: a zero is an
   ending and a one is a beginning; the odd amount can't be split, so the blessing stays whole; and the extra
   rupee is a small debt, an unspoken "we will meet again".
   - https://www.thequint.com/news/india/curious-case-of-the-one-rupee-why-its-in-your-shagun-envelope
   - https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/lifestyle/7-reasons-why-indians-add-one-rupee-in-money-gifting-599818
   - https://dnaindia.com/lifestyle/report-diwali-2024-why-do-we-add-rs-1-coin-in-shagun-envelope-3115672/amp

## Voice-over (~225 words, ~61 s at 3.7 words/s)

**Hook**
Grandma will spend three days feeding you, and thirty seconds trying to pay you for it.

**Setting**
Sunday evening. Bags packed. The car is running. Surveys say more than eighty percent of grandparents give their
grandkids cash. The rest just haven't caught you yet.

**Action (four beats, each bigger)**
It starts with a handshake. A note folded smaller than a stamp, pressed into your palm.
You say, "No, Grandma." You give it back.
Refuse once, and it's in your shirt pocket.
Refuse twice, it's in your bag, between the socks.
Refuse three times, and she hands it to the driver, with instructions.

**Snap**
The car pulls away. You wave. You won.

**Deadpan double line**
Luckily, you made the driver give it back.
Unluckily, she had already hidden five hundred and one rupees inside your lunch box.

**Explanation (the idea + everyday analogy)**
Why five hundred and one? In India, gifts like this end in a one, never a zero.
A zero is an ending. A one is a beginning.
That extra rupee is a tiny loan, like a library book you haven't returned yet.
You have to come back.

**Consequence**
So the money was never the gift. The one rupee was your ticket home.

**Callback twist**
Next month you visit, and give her the rupee back.
She smiles, and slips you one thousand and one.

## Cast (all original)
- **Grandma (Nani):** small, round, white hair in a bun, round glasses, green saree with a gold border, shawl.
- **Grandkid (us, ~20):** hoodie, backpack, sneakers. Our point of view in most shots.
- **Driver:** moustache, cap, leans out of a little yellow-and-green taxi.
- **Mum:** in the back seat, pretending not to watch.

## World
A small Indian town lane at golden hour: pastel two-storey houses with flat roofs, balconies, a blue door,
potted tulsi plant, a neem tree, a parked taxi, rangoli at the doorstep, kites in the sky. Day light for the
lunch-box reveal and the next-month visit.

## Shot outline (rough, final timing comes from the voice)
1. Hook: close-up of Grandma's hands folding a note tiny; she looks up with a sweet smile. Flash.
2. Wide golden lane: taxi idling, bags being loaded.
3. Handshake insert, the note passes palm to palm. Kid shakes head, hands it back.
4. Shirt pocket: quick cut, the note is in the pocket, Grandma innocent.
5. Bag zip: note between socks.
6. Taxi window: Grandma whispers to the driver, who nods gravely.
7. Snap: the taxi drives off down the lane, kid waving from the window (impact kick when the car goes).
8. Deadpan: the driver hands the note back over his shoulder; then cut to the kid opening the lunch box, a 501 note
   folded on top of the parathas.
9. Explanation: Grandma at her door at sunset, a giant glowing "1" coin card beside her; library-book card.
10. Consequence: the kid on the bus home, holding the one-rupee coin up to the light.
11. Callback: next month, day light, the kid gives the coin back; Grandma beams and presses a bigger fold into the
    hand. Final push-in on her smile.
