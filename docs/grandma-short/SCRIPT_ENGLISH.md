# The Goodbye Money: English version (draft v1)

Same story beats as the Indian version. English town, English Grandma, pounds and a lucky penny.

## Facts
1. Saga (UK, Christmas 2019): about half of grandparents planned to give their grandchildren cash, and 72 % of them
   said it was because they didn't know what else to buy.
   https://www.blackpoolgazette.co.uk/lifestyle/half-grandparents-will-give-grandchildren-cash-christmas-1347603
2. An old, widespread superstition: never give an empty purse or wallet. Put a coin inside so money keeps coming
   back. https://folklore.usc.edu/dont-give-empty-wallets/
   https://www.mentalfloss.com/holidays/christmas/20-traditional-gift-giving-superstitions

## Voice-over (~210 words)
Grandma will spend three days feeding you, and thirty seconds trying to pay you for it.
Sunday evening. Bags packed. The car is running.
In one survey, half of grandparents gave their grandkids cash. Most said they didn't know what else to buy. The rest
just haven't caught you yet.
It starts with a handshake. A tenner folded smaller than a stamp, pressed into your palm.
You say, no, Grandma. You give it back.
Refuse once, and it's in your coat pocket.
Refuse twice, it's in your bag, between the socks.
Refuse three times, and she hands it to the driver, with instructions.
The car pulls away. You wave. You won.
Luckily, you made the driver give it back.
Unluckily, she had already hidden twenty pounds and a lucky penny inside your sandwich box.
Why the penny? There's an old rule: never give an empty purse. A coin inside keeps the money coming back.
And that penny is a tiny loan, like a library book you haven't returned yet. You have to come back.
So the money was never the gift. The penny was your ticket home.
Next month you visit, and give her the penny back.
She smiles, and slips you fifty quid.
